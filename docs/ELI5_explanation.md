# Greek Battery Tool Explained Like I'm 5 (ELI5)

## What's a Battery?

Imagine you have a big water tank. When water is cheap and nobody needs it, you fill the tank up. When water is expensive and everyone needs it, you let the water out and sell it. That's basically what a battery does with electricity!

## What's the Greek Tool?

This tool helps people figure out: **"If we put a big battery in Greece that buys and sells electricity every day, how much money could it make?"**

It's like a game where you:
1. Look at old electricity prices from the past
2. Guess the best times to buy (when it's cheap)
3. Guess the best times to sell (when it's expensive)
4. Count how much money you'd make

## The Three Main Parts

### Part 1: Playing Perfect (The Ceiling)
Imagine you have **magic powers** and you KNOW every electricity price before it happens. You can buy at the very cheapest moment and sell at the very most expensive moment. This would make you the MOST money possible.

But here's the thing: nobody has magic powers! So this is just the **highest amount of money** the battery could ever make. Real life will be worse.

### Part 2: Making Guesses (Forecasting)
Without magic powers, you have to make guesses. You look at old price patterns and say: *"Hmm, usually electricity is expensive on Tuesdays around 8 AM, and cheap in the middle of the night."*

The tool tests different guess-making methods:
- **Simple methods** like "prices tomorrow are usually the same as last week"
- **Smart computer methods** that learn from lots of past data

### Part 3: Pretending to Run It (Stress Testing)
The tool also creates **pretend scenarios** like:
- *"What if prices were 30% lower than normal?"*
- *"What if the battery breaks down sometimes?"*
- *"What if the battery gets tired over time?"*

This helps you understand: "Even if things go wrong, could this battery still make money?"

## What It Tells You

The tool tells you things like:
- ✅ With perfect magic powers: €2 million per year
- ✅ With good guesses: €1.2 million per year
- ✅ If prices drop 30%: €800,000 per year
- ✅ After 10 years when the battery is tired: €700,000 per year

## Why It's Not Investment Advice

**Important:** This tool is like a thought experiment. Real electricity markets are way more complicated:
- Other batteries might be competing with you (pushing prices down)
- You might have to follow special rules
- You have taxes and other costs
- The government might help or hurt you

So you shouldn't give money to build a battery just because this tool says it might make money. You need real experts to check many other things first!

## The Bottom Line

**In 3 sentences:**
1. The tool simulates buying and selling electricity every day
2. It shows you the best possible outcome, realistic outcomes, and worst-case outcomes
3. It's a useful starting point to explore the idea, but real life will be more complicated
