# v0.9 design — point-in-time fundamentals forecast benchmark

**Adopted 2 September 2026**, against `main` at `c6c26c4` (v0.8.3). This document is the design of
record for v0.9. Changing it requires a further dated decision entry, as `docs/v0.8_design.md`
requires for v0.8.

**What is landed at adoption.** This design, the two configuration examples it declares
(`config/decision_cutoff.example.json` and `config/fundamentals_geography.example.json`), the test
that keeps both parseable and both refused as declarations, and the project-record entries that
open the milestone. No source code under `src/`, no dependency, no workflow and no data source has
been added, and no accepted figure or analytical behaviour has changed. Section 15 is the phase
sequence that follows; Section 19 holds the gates that can stop it.

**What is not established.** The v0.9.0 source-selection spike has not been run and
`docs/fundamentals_source_assessment_<date>.md` does not exist. Every statement labelled
[Inference] below is therefore unverified, the choice of primary source in Section 20 is a
recommendation and not a decision, and nothing may depend on either until that document converts
each inference into a verified fact or a recorded unknown. Recording this design does not accept a
data source, lift the ADMIE exclusion of 2026-09-01, or admit any figure as evidence.

Labels used throughout:

## 1. Executive recommendation

**v0.9 is feasible, in two unequal halves.** The engineering half — a typed point-in-time feature contract, a declared decision cutoff, an as-of join with an audit table, an availability audit with graded evidence, manifest kinds and a renderer that can carry the result — is feasible with certainty, because every building block already exists in the repository and the repository's own ADMIE timing audit (`src/greek_bess/data/admie_timing.py:1-756`) is a working prototype of the availability policy. The analytical half — a defensible answer to "do accepted fundamentals improve realized dispatch value?" — is feasible only if one candidate source passes a point-in-time availability audit over enough delivery days to say anything. That is not established today for any source.

**Smallest credible version of the milestone.** One accepted source. One variable family. One declared decision-cutoff schedule (no default). One ablation: the existing ridge and histogram-gradient-boosting models on calendar and price-history features (arm A) versus the same two models, same hyperparameters, same refit cadence, same splits, with the accepted point-in-time features appended (arm B). Every arm settled against realized official prices on the same common delivery days under the same battery. Result recorded under two new manifest kinds and rendered by the existing renderer. If accepted-feature coverage is thin, the result is labelled exploratory and general conclusions are refused.

**Largest unknown, and the blocker.** Provable *historical* availability before the cutoff. [Verified] `docs/admie_publication_timing_policy.md:216-225` already states the problem exactly: a provider's timestamp read after the fact is an assertion, only a retrieval before the cutoff is a witness, and witnesses accumulate one delivery day at a time. [Inference] The ENTSO-E Transparency Platform API returns the current value of a datum with no per-datum publication timestamp, so its day-ahead forecasts cannot be proven available historically through the API alone; and its Greek load and renewable forecasts originate from ADMIE, which the 2026-09-01 decision removed from scope (`DECISIONS.md:272-302`). The candidates that *do* carry provable vintages (an archived numerical-weather-prediction run identified by its issue cycle, or an exchange auction result fixed at a known instant) are either heavier to ingest or analytically weaker. Section 3 works through this.

**Why this is worth more than the alternatives.**

- *Another dashboard or one-command demo:* the interactive viewer was declined by dated decision (`DECISIONS.md:97-112`), and `greek-bess demo` already exists (`DECISIONS.md:3-16`). Neither adds evidence.
- *A generic ML model:* `LIMITATIONS.md:99-100` records the one open analytic question in the forecasting layer — "validated weather, demand, fuel, renewable and interconnector forecasts are not yet included". A third price-history-only model answers no question the accepted evidence leaves open; the held-out ML comparison already shows the two existing models within 0.5 percentage points of capture of each other and of `rolling_mean` (`docs/official_multiyear_operational_acceptance_2026-08-27.md:185-198`).
- *A leveraged finance layer or a second revenue stream:* both are excluded until independently validated (`PROMPT.md:120-124`; `AGENTS.md:16-17`), and the external review's revenue-stream item was explicitly deferred by the user (`docs/history/implementation_report_v0.8.3_review_response.md:103-104`).
- *This milestone* tests the one remaining forecasting hypothesis with the project's own evidence standard, and the infrastructure it leaves behind (the point-in-time contract and join) is what any future exogenous input — including a reopened ADMIE track — would have to pass through.

**Three kinds of benefit, kept apart.**

- *Analytical:* a negative or positive answer, on realized settled value, to whether fundamentals help a day-ahead battery in the Greek DAM, with leakage ruled out by construction rather than by assertion.
- *Engineering:* a reusable, tested point-in-time feature contract; a generalized decision-cutoff schedule; an as-of join whose audit table names the revision behind every feature value; graded availability evidence carried into manifests.
- *CV/portfolio:* a demonstrable, testable answer to the interview question "how do you know your backtest did not see the future?", with a concrete leakage sentinel test suite and a documented negative result if that is what the data says.

### 3.2 Notes per candidate the table cannot hold

**ENTSO-E day-ahead forecasts (1-4).** [Verified] The existing client fetches `A44` only (`entsoe.py:111-119`) and parses `revisionNumber` per document (`:233-235`). [Inference] `A65`, `A69`, `A71`, `A61` are available for the Greek zone under the same token and the same XML shape, so client extension is small. [Inference] The API returns the latest stored value; the document's `createdDateTime` is the response creation time, not the original publication instant; the platform's web UI shows an "updated" time per view but there is no documented per-datum publication history. Consequently historical availability can be graded only `assumed` (regulatory deadline) — and Section 4 quarantines that grade. Forward witnessing (a workflow run before the declared cutoff each day) is the only way to accept these, one day at a time. [Verified] The token is not configured today. **Decision conflict:** the GR load, wind and solar forecasts on the platform are submitted by ADMIE; using them is using ADMIE load/RES forecasts. This must be a separate approval track with its own dated decision, and the plan below does not depend on it.

**Regulatory timing (2).** [Inference, to be verified against the published text of Regulation (EU) 543/2013 in v0.9.0] Article 6(1)(b) requires the day-ahead total load forecast no later than two hours before the day-ahead gate closure; Article 14(1)(d) requires the day-ahead wind and solar forecast no later than 18:00 Brussels time on D-1, which is *after* a midday gate. If confirmed, the regulation alone cannot make a wind/solar day-ahead forecast a pre-gate feature; only a witnessed retrieval can.

**NOAA GFS archive (8).** [Inference] The AWS Open Data bucket holds each GFS cycle (00/06/12/18 UTC) under a key carrying the cycle date and hour, with `.idx` sidecars that allow byte-range retrieval of individual GRIB2 messages; 0.25° output is hourly to 120 h; surface downward shortwave radiation, 10 m wind components and 2 m temperature are present; objects have an upload `Last-Modified` header. The archive start (early 2021) leaves the first months of the accepted history (from 1 November 2020) without features; that is disclosed, not filled. Radiation fields are time-averaged over reset buckets and must be de-averaged correctly — a parser-acceptance item, not a detail. Raw volume is large (of the order of 10^2 GB across the history) so raw bytes are hashed and discarded; the reproducibility claim rests on the public archive persisting, which is disclosed. Decoding needs an ecCodes binding; [Inference] recent `eccodes` wheels bundle the library, but this must be proven on the CI image for 3.12 and 3.13 before the source is chosen.

**EEX auction results (12).** [Inference] Weekday primary auctions clear late morning CET and results are published as downloadable reports shortly after. A midday CET gate leaves too small a margin to admit the same-day result under a strict policy, so the rule would be "latest auction whose declared publication instant is strictly before the cutoff", which in practice means D-2. A carbon price moves the price *level*; the accepted evidence shows a battery earns from *spread* (`DECISIONS.md:654-683`), so the honest expectation is little or no dispatch-value change. That is still a valid negative result, and the source exercises every part of the contract with no new dependency and no secret.

**Fuel (13).** [Inference] No free, licensed, primary daily TTF series with a documented publication rule exists. Quarantined unless the operator declares a licensed feed, in which case the schema accepts it without code change to the contract.

### 3.3 Recommended source, and why

[Recommendation] **Primary: NOAA GFS forecast vintages** (candidate 8), variables: surface downward shortwave radiation, 10 m wind speed (from U/V), 2 m temperature, at a declared set of Greek grid points with declared aggregation weights. It is the only candidate that combines provable historical vintages, a public-domain licence, no secret, no conflict with the ADMIE decision, and first-order relevance to the solar-driven midday negative prices and evening peaks the accepted history shows growing since 2023 (`README.md:20-35`).

[Recommendation] **Fallback: EEX EU ETS auction results** (candidate 12), selected automatically if the v0.9.0 spike fails any of three checks: archive coverage across 2021-2026, byte-range subsetting through the `.idx` sidecars, and a pip-installable ecCodes binding passing the four gates on the CI image.

[Recommendation] **Track B (separate approval):** ENTSO-E `A65`/`A69`/`A61` for GR, forward-witnessed only, opened only by a new dated decision that names the reversal of the 2026-09-01 entry, and only after the token is restored.

## 4. Decision-time and gate-closure contract

### 4.1 The cutoff is declared, never defaulted

[Verified] The repository refuses to supply the Greek DAM gate closure on the operator's behalf (`DECISIONS.md:428-448`; `config/admie_gate_closure.example.json` carries a placeholder `reference` that must be replaced). [Recommendation] v0.9 keeps that refusal and reuses the format: a `decision_cutoff` schedule is a `GateClosureSchedule` — one or more dated regimes with `effective_from_delivery_day`, `closure_day_offset` (non-positive), `closure_local_time`, `closure_timezone` from `{Europe/Athens, Europe/Brussels, UTC}`, and a required `reference` citing the rulebook section and effective dates (`admie_timing.py:102-180`). Every v0.9 command that builds features, audits availability or runs the benchmark takes `--decision-cutoff <schedule.json>` as a **required** argument. There is no constant, no environment fallback and no "SDAC default". [Inference] The SDAC common gate is widely described as 12:00 CET/CEST on D-1; that sentence is context for the operator, not a value the tool may assume.

A second declared quantity: **`decision_lead_minutes`**, required, may be `0`. The effective cutoff for delivery day D is `closure_utc(D) − decision_lead_minutes`. This exists because a bidder must build a forecast, plan and submit before the gate; how long that takes is a judgment. Making it required with no default follows the availability-baseline precedent (`DECISIONS.md:527-552`).

### 4.2 Comparison rule

For each feature observation and delivery interval:

```
available_for(D) := published_at_utc < cutoff_utc(D)         (strict; a tie is late)
```

with `cutoff_utc(D)` resolved through the regime in force for D (`GateClosureSchedule.regime_for`, `admie_timing.py:220-231`), refusing a delivery day earlier than the first regime and a closure falling in a DST gap or repetition (`:150-171`). Comparisons are between timezone-aware UTC `pd.Timestamp` values only; a naive timestamp anywhere is refused (`admie_timing.py:481-491` precedent).

### 4.3 The three instants, and which one decides

| Instant | Meaning | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `published_at_utc` | The earliest instant the provider states the datum was available (an exchange result time, an object upload time, a document publication time) | **Decides availability**, subject to the evidence grade |
| `retrieved_at_utc` | When this project's own retrieval observed the datum | Witnesses availability when `retrieved_at_utc < cutoff_utc(D)`; must satisfy `published_at_utc ≤ retrieved_at_utc`, else the row is refused (`admie_timing.py:441-446` precedent) |
| Provider revision instant / `source_revision` | Identity of a re-publication of the same datum | Selects the **decision-time revision**: latest revision with `published_at_utc < cutoff`; later revisions are counted and excluded (`admie_timing.py:652-654` precedent) |

`valid_from`/`valid_to` are *delivery* coordinates (the interval the value describes), not availability coordinates; they are carried as `delivery_start_utc`/`delivery_end_utc` and never used in the availability comparison. `forecast_issue_time_utc` (the model cycle) is a provenance field; it may be *earlier* than `published_at_utc` and never substitutes for it.

[Recommendation] Use the combination: `published_at_utc` decides; `retrieved_at_utc` grades; (`source_document_id`, `source_revision`, `raw_sha256`) identify the exact bytes behind the value.

### 4.4 Evidence grades

Reusing the ADMIE vocabulary (`admie_timing.py:77-82`) and extending it by one grade:

| Grade | Definition | Admitted to the accepted benchmark? |
|---|---|---|
| `witnessed` | `retrieved_at_utc < cutoff_utc(D)` and `published_at_utc < cutoff_utc(D)` | Yes |
| `provider_declared` | `published_at_utc < cutoff_utc(D)` from provider metadata that identifies this datum (auction report time, object upload time, document publication time); retrieval after the cutoff | Yes, reported separately and never merged with `witnessed` |
| `assumed` | Availability inferred from a rule not attached to the datum (a regulatory deadline, a model cycle plus nominal latency, a settlement convention with no per-datum stamp) | **No.** Quarantined; usable only in an explicitly labelled exploratory run that is never recorded as accepted |
| `unavailable` | `published_at_utc ≥ cutoff_utc(D)`, or `published_at_utc` unknown | No; the feature is absent for D |

The precedent for admitting the provider-declared grade while never promoting it is `DECISIONS.md:450-466`.

### 4.5 Delayed, corrected and metadata-less publications

- **Delayed publication:** a datum whose only revision is post-cutoff makes the feature `unavailable` for D. The day is excluded from arm B and, to keep the comparison like-for-like, from arm A on the common-day set; the exclusion is attributed by cause in the summary, following the structural-exclusion practice (`DECISIONS.md:924-932`).
- **Corrected publication with a new revision identity:** handled by decision-time revision selection.
- **Corrected publication with the same identity and different bytes:** refused as an in-place replacement (`admie_timing.py:298-303` precedent). The operator must declare which copy is authoritative in the acceptance document.
- **Restated `published_at_utc` for the same identity:** refused (`:304-310` precedent).
- **Missing `published_at_utc`:** the row cannot enter the accepted table. It may be retained in a quarantine table with grade `assumed` only if an explicit `--admit-assumed-grade` flag is passed, and any output produced that way carries `evidence_grades_admitted` including `assumed` and a result label saying "exploratory, not accepted".
- **Refusal conditions (raise, no partial output):** naive timestamps; `published_at_utc > retrieved_at_utc`; delivery day before the first regime; DST-ambiguous closure; unknown `closure_timezone`; a schedule file absent; a schedule whose `reference` is the example placeholder text.

## 5. Point-in-time feature schema

[Recommendation] A new module `src/greek_bess/data/point_in_time.py` defines `POINT_IN_TIME_COLUMNS`, `ensure_point_in_time(frame)` (validate, normalize, order, copy — the `ensure_canonical` pattern at `schema.py:53-105`), `read_point_in_time_csv`, and the enums below. Timestamps are normalized to nanoseconds like the price schema (`schema.py:40`).

### 5.1 Columns

| Column | Type | Required | Meaning and validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `delivery_start_utc` | tz-aware UTC | yes | Start of the delivery interval the value describes |
| `delivery_end_utc` | tz-aware UTC | yes | Exclusive end; `end > start`; `(end−start)` must equal `resolution_minutes` |
| `market_day` | date | yes | CET/CEST date of `delivery_start_utc` (`MARKET_TZ`); derived, then verified, never supplied alone |
| `source` | str | yes | Closed set: `{"noaa_gfs", "eex_auction", "entsoe_fundamentals", "synthetic"}` — extended only by a reviewed change, as `SUPPORTED_SOURCES` is |
| `dataset` | str | yes | Provider dataset identifier (`gfs.0p25`, `eua_primary_auction`, `A65_A01`) |
| `variable` | str | yes | Closed registry per source with its unit (5.3) |
| `area` | str | yes | Bidding zone `GR`, or a declared geography identifier from the geography config (never a bare coordinate) |
| `unit` | str | yes | Must equal the registry unit for `variable`; mismatch refuses |
| `resolution_minutes` | int | yes | Native resolution of the datum: `{15, 60, 180, 1440}` |
| `value` | float | yes | Finite. **A missing value is an absent row, never `NaN`** |
| `published_at_utc` | tz-aware UTC | yes in the accepted table | See 4.3; absent → quarantine table only |
| `retrieved_at_utc` | tz-aware UTC | yes | ≥ `published_at_utc` |
| `source_document_id` | str | yes | Stable identity of the publication: S3 key, report filename, ENTSO-E `mRID` |
| `source_revision` | int or str | optional | Provider revision; `None` when the provider has none |
| `raw_sha256` | str | yes | Digest of the exact bytes the value was decoded from (a GRIB2 message, a workbook, an XML document) |
| `forecast_issue_time_utc` | tz-aware UTC | optional | Model cycle / issue time for forecasts; must be ≤ `published_at_utc` |
| `forecast_horizon_minutes` | int | optional | `delivery_start_utc − forecast_issue_time_utc`; derived, verified |
| `availability_evidence_grade` | str | yes | `{witnessed, provider_declared, assumed, unavailable}` (4.4) |
| `availability_evidence_detail` | str | optional | What produced the grade (e.g. `s3_last_modified`, `auction_report_timestamp`, `retrieval_before_cutoff`) |

### 5.2 Keys, ordering, duplicates, revisions

- **Uniqueness key:** (`source`, `dataset`, `variable`, `area`, `delivery_start_utc`, `source_document_id`, `source_revision`). Two rows with this key and identical (`value`, `raw_sha256`, `published_at_utc`) are one observation and are de-duplicated. Two rows with this key and any difference are refused with both rows named.
- **Canonical ordering:** `delivery_start_utc`, `variable`, `area`, `published_at_utc`, `source_document_id`, `source_revision`. Ordering is applied by `ensure_point_in_time`, so a table compares equal regardless of input order (property-tested).
- **Revision selection is not done in the schema.** The table holds every revision; the join (Section 6) selects the decision-time revision per delivery day. This is the `--all-revisions` lesson (`docs/admie_publication_timing_policy.md:233-245`): the storage layer must not discard the evidence that a revision was superseded.

### 5.3 Unit registry and validation

A closed mapping `VARIABLE_UNITS`, e.g. `dswrf_surface → W/m2`, `wind_speed_10m → m/s`, `temperature_2m → K`, `eua_auction_clearing_price → EUR/tCO2`, `load_forecast → MW`. `ensure_point_in_time` refuses a row whose `unit` differs from the registry; there is no silent conversion. Range sanity bounds per variable are checked in acceptance (Section 7), not in the schema.

### 5.4 Missing data, resolution transitions, DST

- **Missing:** absent rows. The acceptance stage reports coverage per (`variable`, `market_day`) against the expected interval count from `market_day_starts` at the feature's native resolution. Nothing is forward-filled, interpolated or imputed. This mirrors "never silently interpolate missing official prices" (`AGENTS.md:22`).
- **Resolution transition (1 October 2025):** features keep their native resolution. The join maps a feature to a price interval by containment of the price interval's start in the feature's `[delivery_start_utc, delivery_end_utc)` and records `resolution_relation ∈ {equal, broadcast_coarser_feature}` on the audit row. A coarser feature repeated over four quarter-hour intervals is a broadcast, flagged as such; it is not interpolation because no value is invented. A *finer* feature than the price interval (none proposed) would require a declared aggregation rule and is refused until one exists.
- **DST:** all keys are UTC instants, so 23/25-hour and 92/100-quarter-hour days are handled by construction; `market_day` is derived from `MARKET_TZ`. Any feature defined on a wall-clock label rather than a UTC instant is refused at ingestion. Property tests over 2015-2035 (Section 11) cover the transition days.

### 5.5 Provenance requirements

Every accepted feature table is written beside: a retrieval manifest (`write_retrieval_manifest`) listing every raw document with its `sha256`, `published_at_utc`, `retrieved_at_utc` and coverage; the decision-cutoff schedule used; the geography config used; and a `.summary.json` recording row counts by grade, coverage by variable and market day, revision statistics, and the digests of the schedule and geography files. The accepted table's own SHA-256 is what the benchmark manifests later declare.

## 6. Point-in-time join algorithm

[Recommendation] Module `src/greek_bess/forecast/point_in_time_join.py`, one public function `join_point_in_time_features(prices, features, *, schedule, decision_lead_minutes, admitted_grades)` returning `(feature_frame, audit_table, summary)`.

### 6.1 Pseudocode

```
INPUT
  prices      : canonical price frame (ensure_canonical; assess_quality must be valid)
  features    : point-in-time frame (ensure_point_in_time), all revisions present
  schedule    : GateClosureSchedule (declared; no default)
  lead        : decision_lead_minutes (declared; no default)
  admitted    : subset of {witnessed, provider_declared}; 'assumed' only under the
                explicit exploratory flag, and then the summary says so

PRECONDITIONS (raise on failure)
  every features.published_at_utc and retrieved_at_utc tz-aware
  features.published_at_utc <= features.retrieved_at_utc
  no (uniqueness-key) conflicts
  every price market_day >= first regime's effective_from_delivery_day

FOR each market_day D in prices (ascending):
    cutoff := schedule.closure_utc(D) - lead                      # may raise: DST gap/repeat
    price_intervals := prices rows with market_day == D
    FOR each (variable, area) in the declared feature set:
        candidates := features rows where
                        variable, area match
                        and delivery interval overlaps some interval of D
                        and published_at_utc < cutoff               # STRICT
                        and grade in admitted
        IF candidates is empty:
            mark (D, variable, area) as UNAVAILABLE with reason ∈
              {no_publication, all_publications_after_cutoff, grade_not_admitted}
            CONTINUE
        # decision-time revision: per delivery interval, the latest published strictly
        # before the cutoff; ties broken by source_revision then source_document_id
        selected := for each delivery interval of the feature: argmax over candidates
                    by (published_at_utc, source_revision, source_document_id)
        superseded_after_cutoff := count of rows for (variable, area, D) with
                                   published_at_utc >= cutoff
        FOR each price interval p in price_intervals:
            f := selected row whose [delivery_start, delivery_end) contains p.delivery_start
            IF f is None:  mark (D, variable, area) as INCOMPLETE; BREAK
            emit feature value f.value into feature_frame at (p.delivery_start_utc, column)
            emit audit row:
              (D, p.delivery_start_utc, variable, area, cutoff, f.published_at_utc,
               f.retrieved_at_utc, f.source_document_id, f.source_revision, f.raw_sha256,
               f.forecast_issue_time_utc, lead_minutes = cutoff - f.published_at_utc,
               grade, resolution_relation, superseded_after_cutoff)
    IF any (variable, area) for D is UNAVAILABLE or INCOMPLETE:
        the whole day D is excluded from arm B, with every reason recorded;
        no partial feature vector is ever emitted for a day

POSTCONDITIONS (assert; a violation is a defect, not a finding)
  every audit row has published_at_utc < cutoff
  every feature value in feature_frame has exactly one audit row
  no feature column contains a value for a delivery interval of a day marked excluded
  audit table is deterministic under any permutation of `features` rows
OUTPUT
  feature_frame : one row per price interval, columns = declared feature names, NaN only
                  on excluded days (and those days are listed, never imputed)
  audit_table   : as above; written beside the features; never contains a price
  summary       : day counts by status and reason, grade counts, lead-time min/median,
                  superseded counts, schedule_id, lead, digests of inputs
```

### 6.2 Invariants

1. **Strict cutoff.** `published_at_utc < cutoff_utc(D)`; equality is late (`docs/admie_publication_timing_policy.md:212-214`).
2. **No target-day realised value can enter.** Realised observations are typed as `dataset` values with `forecast_issue_time_utc = None` and `published_at_utc` after delivery; the strict cutoff excludes them mechanically for D. A test additionally injects a realised-price feature and asserts refusal by *name* — the join refuses any feature whose `variable` is in a `REALISED_PRICE_VARIABLES` deny-list regardless of timestamps, so the label can never be smuggled in under a timestamp error.
3. **One revision per value, and it is the decision-time one.** Later revisions are counted, never used.
4. **No silent fill.** A day is complete or excluded. The excluded-day list is part of the summary and of the manifest.
5. **Permutation invariance.** Output does not depend on feature row order or on the order sources were retrieved.
6. **Audit completeness.** Every feature value is traceable to (`source_document_id`, `source_revision`, `raw_sha256`).
7. **Fails clearly on ambiguous metadata.** Missing/naive `published_at_utc`, a DST-ambiguous cutoff, a delivery day before the first regime, or a uniqueness conflict all raise before any output is written.

## 8. Forecast benchmark design

### 8.1 Arms

| Arm | Methods | Features | Code path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baselines | `daily_persistence`, `weekly_persistence`, `rolling_mean`, `ensemble` | price history only | `naive.py` unchanged |
| A (control) | `ridge`, `hist_gradient_boosting` | `FEATURE_COLUMNS` (`ml.py:47`) | `ml.py` unchanged |
| B (challenger) | `ridge_fundamentals`, `hist_gradient_boosting_fundamentals` | `FEATURE_COLUMNS` + accepted point-in-time feature columns | same `_walk_forward_predict`/`_make_model`, with `feature_columns` passed |

No deep learning: the accepted evidence shows the two existing model families already at the ceiling the features allow (capture 0.82 versus 0.82 for a naïve `rolling_mean`), so the open question is the *information*, not the *function class*. A third family would confound the ablation.

### 8.2 Protocol (rules, not dates)

- **Splits.** Training, validation and held-out test are contiguous, time-ordered blocks of market days. Rules: (i) the test block is the latest block; (ii) validation is one full year of market days, or the longest contiguous block available below one year when the accepted feature coverage cannot support a year, with the shortfall disclosed; (iii) training starts at the later of the first accepted-feature market day plus the warm-up the price features need (28 days, `MLForecastConfig.feature_window_days`) and the first price day; (iv) the test block must contain at least one full meteorological season of quarter-hour delivery days for a result to be labelled anything but exploratory. The accepted history holds hourly delivery from 1 November 2020 to 30 September 2025 and quarter-hour from 1 October 2025 (`STATUS.md:327-329`); if the accepted feature coverage begins in early 2021, the prior acceptance's split (validation 2024, test 2025-01-01 onward, `official_multiyear_operational_acceptance_2026-08-27.md:124-128`) remains admissible and is the recommended first candidate, subject to rule (iii).
- **Same days for A and B.** The evaluation set is the intersection of days complete for every baseline, arm A and arm B (`ml_dispatch.py:46-69` pattern). Days excluded because a feature was unavailable are attributed by cause. Arm A is evaluated on the same reduced set, so a difference cannot come from a different calendar.
- **Refit cadence.** `refit_frequency_days = 7`, forced refit on the first test day, expanding training window — identical to the accepted run. Both arms refit on the same days.
- **Hyperparameter boundary.** No new tuning in v0.9. Arm B uses arm A's fixed hyperparameters (`MLForecastConfig` defaults, `ml.py:60-69`). Selection between the two B models uses validation RMSE only, exactly as today (`ml.py:190-195`); test metrics never select. If a later milestone tunes anything, it tunes on validation only and records the grid.
- **Scaling.** Inside the pipeline, fitted on the training rows of each refit only (`ml.py:368-386`).
- **Categorical handling.** None introduced. Calendar features stay cyclical; the feature set adds only numeric fundamentals.
- **Seeds.** `random_seed = 42` recorded; HGB is deterministic given it; ridge is deterministic.
- **Model-artifact policy.** No fitted model is persisted (`.gitignore:33-40` excludes `*.joblib`, `*.pkl`, `models/`). Refit logs, feature provenance and the feature-set digest are recorded instead.
- **1 October 2025 transition.** Disclosed, not corrected, as today (`LIMITATIONS.md:101-104`). Additionally: metrics reported for hourly-era and quarter-hour-era test days separately; broadcast counts reported.
- **Incomplete days.** Excluded and listed; never imputed.
- **Missing features.** In the test/validation windows, a day with any missing accepted feature is excluded from every arm (common-day rule). In training, rows on such days are dropped from arm B's fit (count recorded); arm A trains on its own complete rows. The `SimpleImputer` remains only for the price-lag warm-up `NaN`s it exists for today; it must not receive fundamentals — enforce by asserting no `NaN` in fundamentals columns before fit.
- **Feature provenance.** A `FUNDAMENTALS_FEATURE_PROVENANCE` mapping, one sentence per column, in the style of `ml.py:28-46`, e.g. `dswrf_surface_gr: "GFS surface downward shortwave radiation, decision-time cycle, published strictly before the declared cutoff; grade recorded per day"`.

## 9. Evaluation design

### 9.1 Price-accuracy metrics (secondary)

Per arm and per method, computed by `calculate_forecast_metrics` (`naive.py:153-223`, unchanged): MAE, RMSE, mean error (bias), median absolute error, WAPE, correlation, negative-price precision and recall, coverage. Additionally sliced: by delivery year (market clock, the `annual.py` convention), by interval of day (market-clock slot), by price regime (declared bands, e.g. ≤ 0, 0-50, 50-150, > 150 EUR/MWh — declared in the benchmark config, not defaulted), and by resolution era. MAPE stays excluded (`METHODOLOGY.md:126-127`).

### 9.2 Operational metrics (primary)

Per method on the common days, from `_backtest_precomputed_forecast` (`ml_dispatch.py:153-299`): realised settled gross margin, perfect-foresight regret, capture ratio, loss-making days, equivalent cycles, grid charge and discharge (turnover). New, recorded by the producing module and never by the renderer: **incremental realised margin of each B model versus its own A counterpart** and versus each baseline, on the common days; the paired daily difference series written to CSV; the count of common days on which B settled higher than A.

### 9.3 Which metric is primary, and why

[Recommendation] **Primary: realised settled gross margin on the common days, expressed as the incremental margin of B over A for the same model family.** The project's question is dispatch value, not price error; the accepted evidence shows the two diverge (`rolling_mean` has worse RMSE than `ridge` and captures more value, `official_multiyear_operational_acceptance_2026-08-27.md:200-203`). RMSE is reported because it explains the mechanism, not because it decides.

### 9.4 Equivalence guarantees

All arms share: the same delivery days (intersection); the same `BatteryDispatchConfig` (asserted identical by value, and recorded as the `equivalent_basis` in the summary, following `stress/ensemble.py`'s basis check); the same availability (scalar 1.0 or the same declared schedule); the same fees and degradation cost (fields of the config); the same initial and terminal SOC (enforced by `_backtest_precomputed_forecast`, `ml_dispatch.py:160-166`); the same realised prices (asserted by the identical perfect-foresight ceiling across methods, `ml_dispatch.py:88-93`, and by a settlement restatement test).

## 10. Statistical interpretation

**What may be concluded.** On this one held-out period, under this battery, this cutoff and these accepted features, model B settled X euro more (or less) than model A on N common days. Slices by year, slot and regime describe where that difference sat. That is a historical benchmark outcome.

**What may not.** That the difference will recur; that it is a rate; that any figure is expected revenue; anything about probability of loss; anything about a different battery, cutoff, or market regime (the replayed history predates battery competition, `LIMITATIONS.md:14-18`). One split is one draw from a non-stationary history and is not a distribution.

**Optimistic bias.** Two sources, both present today and both to be named in the report: (i) selecting the "best" B model on validation and reporting it on test is fair, but reporting the best of *several* B variants or feature subsets on test would not be — v0.9 therefore runs exactly one feature set and exactly the two fixed models; (ii) any decision made after seeing test results (changing the cutoff, the geography, the split) contaminates the test. The plan forbids re-splitting after a first test run (Section 19).

**Uncertainty intervals.** [Recommendation] **Excluded from the accepted v0.9 result.** A dependence-preserving option exists — a moving-block bootstrap of the paired daily margin differences, or a Diebold-Mariano-type comparison with a heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent variance — and either is a statement about sampling variability of a *sample statistic*, not a market probability. But the repository's distributional vocabulary rules (`stress/ensemble.py:80-97`) and the 2026-08-27 decision (`DECISIONS.md:891-909`) make adding one a separate, deliberate decision with its own wording, not a v0.9 side effect. v0.9 reports the paired daily differences and their sign counts, which a reader can inspect without a probabilistic claim.

**Wording for result labels** (the `result_label` each summary carries):

- Feature availability audit: *"Point-in-time availability audit of declared fundamentals against a declared decision cutoff; data-acceptance evidence about availability and revision timing only, not a forecast, a skill claim or investment evidence."*
- Forecast benchmark: *"Leakage-safe walk-forward Greek DAM fundamentals ablation on one held-out period; research benchmark of price error, not expected investment revenue."*
- Dispatch comparison: *"Held-out like-for-like Greek DAM fundamentals forecast-dispatch comparison settled at realized prices on common days; historical research result, not expected investment revenue."*
- Exploratory suffix, appended when any gate in Section 19 downgrades the run: *"…; exploratory: accepted-feature coverage does not support a general conclusion."*

## 18. CV deliverables

**CV bullet.** "Designed and built a leakage-safe, point-in-time fundamentals benchmark for Greek day-ahead battery dispatch: declared decision cutoffs, revision-aware as-of joins with per-value provenance, walk-forward validation, constrained MILP dispatch settled at realised prices, and a property-tested CI suite; reported a documented positive/negative result on realised value."

**Extended portfolio description.** A research tool over the official Greek Day-Ahead Market history (2020-2026) compares a perfect-foresight ceiling with causal forecast backtests for a standalone battery. v0.9 asks whether independently validated exogenous fundamentals improve realised dispatch value over price-history models. Every feature value used for a delivery interval is provably available before a declared decision cutoff: the system stores all publication revisions, selects the decision-time revision, grades availability evidence as witnessed or provider-declared, and quarantines anything weaker. Baselines and challengers are refit walk-forward on identical days, planned through the same constrained optimiser, and settled against the same realised prices under identical physical and terminal-energy constraints. Results are recorded under a versioned manifest contract that refuses unlabelled or distributional claims, and rendered by a read-only report.

**Architecture-diagram outline.** Official prices (HEnEx/ENTSO-E, custodied) → canonical UTC interval schema → [new] fundamentals source → point-in-time table (all revisions, published/retrieved instants, hashes) → declared decision-cutoff schedule → availability audit (grades) → as-of join + audit table → causal feature table (A) / augmented (B) → walk-forward ridge/HGB → daily forecast-planned dispatch (MILP, relaxation-first) → settlement at realised prices → like-for-like comparison → run manifests (closed kinds, labels, standing exclusions) → static report. Side rails: custody records, encrypted release copies, CI gates.

**Benchmark table template** (values transcribed from the recorded manifest; slices repeated per era):

| Method | Arm | Common days | RMSE (EUR/MWh) | Realised margin (EUR) | PF ceiling (EUR) | Capture | Incremental vs A (EUR) | Evidence grades admitted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Defensible headline template.** "On N common held-out delivery days ([dates]), with features graded [witnessed: n₁, provider-declared: n₂] against a declared cutoff of [schedule id, lead], the fundamentals-augmented [model] settled EUR X [more/less] than the same model on price history alone under an identical 50 MW / 100 MWh battery and identical realised prices; capture [a] versus [b] against a common perfect-foresight ceiling of EUR C. This is a historical benchmark on one period, not expected revenue."

**Interview talking points.** Why a strict cutoff and why a tie is late; witnessed versus provider-declared versus assumed, and why the grades never merge; why the store keeps every revision; why value, not RMSE, is primary and the one accepted case where they disagree; why a daily terminal-energy convention is the only fair comparison; what the relaxation-first solve proves and where it fails; why probability language is refused and what it would take to admit it; what a negative result taught.

**Claims that must not be made.** Expected revenue; probability of loss; that fundamentals "will" improve value; that the benchmark is investment evidence; that a witnessed day count under a season supports a general conclusion; that ADMIE forecasts were used; that any accepted price-history figure changed.
